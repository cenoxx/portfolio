How I got here

My interest in technology started long before my career did. Growing up, computers were always around me.

My dad was constantly working on computers and hardware, and my grandfather even owned a computer store in Troy, Ohio called Trojan Business Supply. Being surrounded by technology sparked an early curiosity that eventually led me to build and repair computers of my own. Before long, I became the friend people called when something wasn't working.

In high school, I attended the Upper Valley Career Center in Piqua, where I earned a career technical certificate in Information Technology. The program focused heavily on hardware, networking, and technical support, though it also introduced software development through C++. At the time, programming wasn't what interested me most. I was far more interested in the hardware side of technology and understanding how computers worked.

After high school, I landed an entry-level Solution Architect position with an IT sales business. From there, I spent several years working my way up through a variety of technology roles, gaining experience in everything from technical support to IT infrastructure. Technology had always been a passion of mine, but software development still wasn't something I had seriously considered as a career.

Eventually, I joined Copeland as a Manufacturing IT Systems Analyst, which was a fancy way of saying I was the plant IT guy. My role involved supporting users, maintaining systems, troubleshooting problems, and helping keep manufacturing operations running. While I enjoyed the work, I didn't realize it would be the position that would completely change the direction of my career.

That turning point came when I was assigned a project involving Inductive Automation's Ignition SCADA platform. As part of the project, I began writing Python scripts and developing custom functionality for the business. What started as a simple assignment quickly became something much bigger. For the first time, I wasn't just supporting technology. I was building it.

The challenge of creating software and solving problems through code hooked me almost immediately. The more I learned, the more I wanted to build. What began as a small manufacturing software project eventually opened the door to a transition into software development, where I found the kind of work I had been looking for all along.

Today, I work as a Senior Software Developer building enterprise applications and software solutions for manufacturing environments. After several years in development, my passion for creating technology continues to grow. Outside of my professional work, I've started focusing heavily on game development, combining my interest in software engineering with my lifelong love of games and interactive experiences.

Looking back, it's funny that the part of technology I was least interested in during high school ended up becoming my career. What started with building PCs and helping friends troubleshoot their computers eventually led me to software development, and now toward game development and other creative technical pursuits. For me, the most rewarding part of technology has always been learning how things work and then using that knowledge to build something of my own.
